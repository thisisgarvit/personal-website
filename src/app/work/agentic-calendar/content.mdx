A concept for an agentic layer that sits on top of the calendar tools you
already use — one that understands your priorities, schedules proactively,
takes actions on your behalf, and knows when to ask first.

## Situation

The brief: imagine a future where the calendar itself is an intelligent
agent, and redesign how people interact with a proactive, autonomous
scheduling system from the ground up.

Today's calendars — including the AI schedulers — still make you do the
work: block focus time, add personal time, enter every task, maintain the
grid by hand. The scan of current players (Reclaim, FlowSavvy, Clockwise,
Todoist, Motion, Toki, Codot, SkedPal, Notion Calendar) plus user-needs
mining on Reddit and blogs shaped where an agent could genuinely differ.

<CaseSection treatment="decision" title="The bet">

Don't build another feature-heavy calendar app. Build an intelligent
agentic layer that works with the tools people already run — Google
Calendar, Notion Calendar, Reclaim — and reduces cognitive load through
close-to-autonomous decision-making.

There is a second, colder reason to ship a layer instead of an app: it
collects real data on user behavior and problems first, so build effort
stays pointed at things that will never go to waste.

</CaseSection>

## The system

Everything an agent does well starts with context. The concept's first
commitment is context-aware intelligence assembled through integrations
with the tools a working week actually touches:

1. **Existing calendars** (Google Calendar, Notion Calendar, tools like
   Reclaim) — meeting, work, and personal commitment patterns; creating and
   managing events.
2. **Slack and Gmail** — conversations, tags, flight bookings, and action
   items that reveal task priority.
3. **Google or Atlassian workspace** (Docs, Jira tickets and roadmaps) —
   what work matters and how much.
4. **Meeting-notes tools like Granola** — action items owed to you, so
   nothing is forgotten.
5. **Apps like Uber and MakeMyTrip** — so the agent can act, such as
   booking the cab before the flight.

On that context, four capabilities:

1. **Intelligent monitoring of goals and priorities.** Instead of forcing
   you to block every minute, the agent learns your natural work rhythms
   and classifies time into creative, operational, and restorative
   timespace — then schedules work into the times it will actually fit.
2. **Autonomous permissions to negotiate on your behalf.** Tell it "this
   meeting will run 30 minutes over and I need two hours after" and it
   reschedules what follows, messaging attendees through mail or Slack —
   prioritized by attendees, agenda, and task priority pulled from the
   integrations. On sick leave, it attends as your proxy or declines
   intelligently. People who want your time talk to your agent, not your
   assistant.
3. **An all-time helper across past, present, and future.** It prepares as
   well as schedules: notes from previous meetings ready before the next
   one, the follow-up drafted after, the cab booked before the flight, and
   agent-to-agent scheduling inside the business with no intervention.
4. **You stay in control of the smart suggestions.** The agent proposes
   improvements based on your observed patterns — and autonomy never means
   losing the wheel.

<CaseSection treatment="constraint" title="Approval boundaries">

The line between "do it" and "ask me" is itself priority-aware: the agent
is expected to know when an action needs approval and when it does not. The
prototype makes that boundary a first-class surface — an Actions tab
listing every agent decision that needs sign-off, each with clear context
and simple approve/decline buttons, keeping a deliberate balance between
automation and user control.

</CaseSection>

## Prototype walkthrough

The [mockups](https://html-starter-sandy-sigma.vercel.app/) — wireframes
generated with Claude, deployed on Vercel — show five tabs in web and
mobile views, plus chat and mic input for talking to the agent in real
time:

1. **Calendar** — a clean day view of AI-optimized blocks from every
   connected calendar, with personal events routed to personal calendars
   and professional events to professional ones.
2. **Actions** — the approval queue described above.
3. **Suggestions** — recommendations from pattern analysis shown as a
   git-style diff, each with its expected benefit; align them at the start
   of the day and follow a conflict-free schedule.
4. **Tasks** — a unified list from Gmail, Jira, Slack, and Notion with
   priority, source, due date, and time estimates, for work not yet on the
   calendar.
5. **Analytics** — quantifying how much time the agent streamlined versus
   manual calendar management.
