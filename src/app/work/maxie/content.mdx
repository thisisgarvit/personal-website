Maxie is a 0→1 concept for an AI browser built for active knowledge work —
contextual memory, trainable agents, and an agent marketplace — rather than
another chat sidebar. It exists as a full product spec and a clickable
prototype; it was never built.

<CaseSection treatment="constraint" title="Problem">

Knowledge workers spend most of their working hours in a browser, yet the
browser is still designed for passive consumption. Even the new AI browsers
— Comet, Dia — have mostly added a chat panel to the side of the same old
surface. Three problems stay unsolved:

1. **Tab chaos and cognitive load.** Users sit on 20+ open tabs with no
   organization beyond willpower, and professionals lose productive time
   every day just relocating information they have already seen.
2. **Information isolation across tools and time.** The browser sits at the
   center of knowledge work but keeps no persistent memory of insights,
   discoveries, or work patterns. Hit a similar problem weeks later and you
   start the research from zero.
3. **No automation.** The web became the primary platform for business
   tools, but browsers offer no way to automate the multi-step workflows
   people repeat daily — checking dashboards, updating spreadsheets,
   monitoring competitor pricing, generating reports.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection treatment="decision" title="The bet">

Treat the browser as a partner with the human in control: it remembers what
you have learned, organizes information contextually, and automates
repetitive workflows under your oversight. The sharper claim underneath:
**agents will replace browser extensions.** Whoever owns the surface where
agents are built, trained, and traded owns the next browser platform.

</CaseSection>

## Assumptions

- **Who.** Knowledge professionals — PMs, analysts, consultants,
  researchers — plus developers and data workers. Jobs to be done:
  summarize, compare, and decide; automate repeated web workflows; extract
  web data; evaluate APIs or a local build; test and monitor.
- **What.** "AI browser" means agentic AI applied to the browsing surface
  itself, not a chat add-on. No direct competitor owns this yet; the
  adjacent field includes Fellou, Comet, Dia, Brave Leo, BrightData's Agent
  Browser, Operator, Sigma, BrowserBase, DoBrowser, and Opera Neon.
- **Why.** Improve retention and open a productive revenue model.
- **When.** No fixed timeline; MVP assumed within 6 months.

## The system

Three systems carry the concept:

1. **Contextual memory and tab management.** The browser auto-groups
   browsing, mail, and documents into projects, each with summaries and
   quick views you can interrogate later — a NotebookLM-style "chat with
   what you visited." A visual browsing tree shows your research path as a
   map, including forecast branches worth visiting next. Context persists
   across time, tools, and sessions.
2. **Agent builder and marketplace.** Agents are built from prompts and
   trained by demonstration — DOM, vision, voice, files, APIs, recorded
   workflows, and device context — asking questions as they learn. Finished
   agents can be shared across an organization or sold on a marketplace,
   one-time or subscription. Example: a frontend developer trains an agent
   on the local build that runs dev tests before each deploy and sends back
   a report.
3. **In-built agents with self or guided training.** A daily assistant
   covers the table stakes — close tabs, summarize pages, answer questions
   in place. A memory agent surfaces relevant material from what you have
   already visited. Agents can be linked: a summarizer chained to a mail
   agent sends a work summary to the right recipients when triggered.

## Provenance and human control

Autonomy without accountability is how a browser loses trust, so control is
specified as a feature, not a caveat:

- Retrieval always shows where a fact came from.
- A policy and safety engine holds allow/deny rules, spending limits,
  content categories, and jurisdictional compliance.
- Verifiable action logs: run logs, reproducible replays, audit trails.
- Agent training on your data is optional, and that data is encrypted to
  your account.

## Differentiation and moat

Maxie's moats compound: the more users train agents, the better the
platform's workflow intelligence becomes; the longer someone browses with
contextual memory, the more expensive switching gets; and the marketplace
carries its own network effects.

## Adoption

Start with a pilot release to validate usefulness and test the experience:
an invite-only beta with a waiting list, aimed at developers, PMs,
analysts, consultants, and researchers in communities where AI adoption is
already high. Go-to-market runs product-led — freemium with the memory and
tab-management features demonstrating value immediately — alongside dev and
research community engagement, enterprise pilots with consulting firms and
SaaS teams to prove ROI, and case-study content on agentic browsers as the
next paradigm. Expansion adds the agent marketplace to hook teams, deep
integrations with productivity tools, and broader marketing to
non-technical professionals once traction is built.

## Pricing hypothesis

Subscription-first, because LLM costs are themselves subscriptions; the
marketplace adds a 20–30% commission on agent sales, plus bulk credits for
internal enterprise marketplaces. Enterprise plans come later as custom
"talk to sales" pricing.
