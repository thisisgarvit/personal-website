# Garvit Portfolio — Underrated Design Reference Scout

Research pass: 17 July 2026. Reference-only round; no design direction has been started.

## Quick shortlist

My top five for a quirky-but-credible product-manager site are:

1. **★ Boris Verks** — the strongest proof that product outcomes can look designed rather than résumé-like.
2. **★ Paco Lui** — the best calibration of serious product work plus one genuinely delightful toy.
3. **★ Alejandro Verdeja** — highly legible product stories with a human creative sidecar.
4. **★ Molly Mielke McCarthy** — the best model for compressed, founder-grade credibility with dry personality.
5. **★ Justin Farrugia** — a sharp startup-operator identity built from small utility interactions.

The ten references fall into four useful direction clusters:

| Direction cluster | Core feeling | Best audience signal | References |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Product Brain, Toy Drawer** | Competent operator with one memorable playful system | Product leads + HR | Paco Lui, Hey Valentin, Gokul Krishnan |
| **B. Operator’s Field Notes** | Outcomes, decisions, velocity and startup fluency | Founders + product leads | Boris Verks, Justin Farrugia, Alejandro Verdeja |
| **C. Editorial Polymath** | Taste, curiosity and a life beyond job titles | HR + design-sensitive founders | Molly Mielke McCarthy, Elizabeth Goodspeed |
| **D. Studio OS / Personal Universe** | A personal site that behaves like a designed system | Creative/product leads | Jin Su Park, Carson Fidje |

## A. Product Brain, Toy Drawer

The safest version of “quirky”: the hierarchy stays conventional and useful, while one contained interaction or illustration creates the smile.

### ★ 1. Paco Lui — Product designer

- **Live:** https://www.pacolui.com/
- **Surfaced via:** [Dark.design](https://www.dark.design/website/paco-lui) and a [2025 personal-site roundup](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/etrofimov_top-10-portfolios-personal-websites-my-activity-7371444876602851328--xkx)
- **Why it is cool:** The page opens with normal trust signals—Toronto, local time, a direct product-design sentence, and current work at Google Chrome—then rewards curiosity with a hand-tracking art canvas. The playful feature is explicitly optional (“Turn on webcam / Maybe later”), so the toy never blocks the portfolio. Tiny jokes such as “why is your monitor so WIDE mate,” “Soon™,” keyboard/Gundam chat bait, symbol-led project names, and a clear “Experiments” lane make the personality feel authored rather than sprinkled on. The selected-work area is still a crisp text-first list with one-sentence problem framing.
- **Lands with:** **Product leads first** (clear craft and experimentation), **HR second** (warm and memorable), **startup founders third** (Google proof plus technical curiosity).
- **Reference value for Garvit:** Best overall calibration. Borrow the containment principle: one optional silly product, not playful friction everywhere.

### 2. Hey Valentin — Product designer & illustrator

- **Live:** https://www.heyvalentin.club/
- **Surfaced via:** [Dark.design](https://www.dark.design/website/hey-valentin)
- **Why it is cool:** An illustrated self-portrait literally waves hello while the intro sentence turns “Valentin,” “Product designer,” and “illustrator” into semantic navigation. The site separates product design, illustration and shop without making the person feel fragmented. Project copy has a relaxed zine voice—“Sometimes it’s great, sometimes not, but it exists”—and tap/toggle galleries make the work feel handled rather than merely displayed. A small banner for Appshot, a tool he made for product designers, quietly upgrades the illustration-heavy identity with builder credibility.
- **Lands with:** **HR first** (instantly personable), **design/product leads second** (range plus a real tool), **founders third** (approachable freelancer energy).
- **Reference value for Garvit:** A good model for a mascot/portrait that is a host, not decoration; also for using a side project as proof of initiative.

### 3. Gokul Krishnan — Product designer

- **Live:** https://gokulkrishnan.com/
- **Surfaced via:** [Dark.design](https://www.dark.design/website/gokul-krishnan)
- **Why it is cool:** A compact status strip (“Oh, hello there,” London, time, weather) creates presence before a large typographic statement cycles between branding, user experience and interactive experiences. Work is labeled with year and discipline, so the gallery reads like an index rather than a pile of mockups. The visual language is deliberately reduced and supported by the memorable principle, “The less it is the cooler is your interface.”
- **Lands with:** **HR first** (polished and instantly legible), **product/design leads second** (disciplined hierarchy), **founders third** (experienced but less outcome-led).
- **Reference value for Garvit:** Useful for a minimal shell with one live-data quirk. The rotating specialties should not replace a crisp PM positioning sentence.

## B. Operator’s Field Notes

These sites make seniority feel tangible through outcomes, role clarity and operational details, then add personality in microcopy or utility behavior.

### ★ 4. Boris Verks — Product designer with product-management experience

- **Live:** https://www.borisverks.com/
- **Surfaced via:** [Minimal Gallery](https://minimal.gallery/boris-verks/) and [Footer.design](https://www.footer.design/sites/boris-verks)
- **Why it is cool:** This is the closest direct analogue for Garvit. Oversized, high-contrast typography gives the page edge, but each role immediately resolves into a plain-language product description and check-marked outcomes: conversion, churn, revenue, release count, app rating and awards. The chronology visibly shows growth from designer to product owner and head of design. Testimonials are unusually specific about big-picture conceptualization, micro-interactions and handling technical constraints. Its bright brutalist/monochrome character keeps a very résumé-dense page from feeling corporate.
- **Lands with:** **Product leads first** (decisions and measurable effects), **startup founders a close second** (0→1, go-to-market, sales and team leadership), **HR third** (seniority is easy to scan).
- **Reference value for Garvit:** Top structural reference. It proves that metrics, role scope and judgment can be the visual material—not an appendix below glossy screenshots.

### ★ 5. Justin Farrugia — Software designer for early-stage startups

- **Live:** https://www.justinfarrugia.com/
- **Surfaced via:** [Dark.design](https://www.dark.design/website/justin-farrugia)
- **Why it is cool:** The site feels like a compact command surface. A dark utility aesthetic supports real actions: press **C** to copy email, book a call, DM on X, see availability, and read current/previous roles without hunting. “Currently” pairs paid work with a self-started Framer resource business, immediately signaling maker/operator energy. The project rail uses tightly cropped product screens and short task-level titles (“Forecasting,” “Onboarding checklist,” “Ask the advisor”) rather than inflated case-study names. Local time and the “Copied email” state are tiny interactions with practical value.
- **Lands with:** **Startup founders first** (available, fast, direct, startup-native), **product leads second** (specific product surfaces), **HR third** (less narrative context, but very polished).
- **Reference value for Garvit:** Strong model for turning contact details and status into interface behavior. The dark palette itself is less important than the sense that every pixel performs a job.

### ★ 6. Alejandro Verdeja — Product designer

- **Live:** https://alejandroverdeja.com/
- **Surfaced via:** a [Reddit UXDesign portfolio-feedback thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/UXDesign/comments/1d1lnmk)
- **Why it is cool:** “I love finding efficiency within complexity” is followed by an oversized name and extremely readable project summaries. Each summary begins with scope and stakes—national bank, Fortune 500 tool used by 5,000+ employees, first insurance app, design-team leadership, 40-day launch—before asking for a click. Credentials sit in the flow instead of on a forgotten résumé page. Then the site changes register with “I also really like to draw,” a sketch gallery, and hand-painted bags inspired by the Labours of Hercules. That creative lane makes him feel like a person without diluting his professional positioning.
- **Lands with:** **HR first** (clear story, credentials, warmth), **product leads second** (scope and complexity are obvious), **founders third** (delivery speed and leadership).
- **Reference value for Garvit:** Excellent model for “serious main quest + charming side quest.” The side interest earns its space because it is specific and well made.

## C. Editorial Polymath

For a PM who wants to signal taste and curiosity rather than cosplay as a visual designer. These sites win through editing, voice and sequencing.

### ★ 7. Molly Mielke McCarthy — Investor, former product designer, writer

- **Live:** https://www.molly.info/
- **Surfaced through:** personal-site and design-community discovery; live site verified directly
- **Why it is cool:** The homepage is essentially one beautifully edited professional paragraph. Instead of a hero slogan plus logo wall, it links each claim in a chronological list: Sequoia, Stripe Press, Figma, Notion, The Browser Company and earlier design work. A parenthetical “(rip),” “I also write and take photos,” a tiny education index and a click-to-copy email add dry humanity without ornament. This is information architecture as status: the confidence comes from leaving almost everything out.
- **Lands with:** **Startup founders first** (dense network/operating credibility), **product leads second** (career narrative shows range and judgment), **HR third** (elegant, though not a conventional case-study portfolio).
- **Reference value for Garvit:** Best copy-density reference. If Garvit’s experience is strong, a compact linked narrative can outperform a decorative logo parade.

### 8. Elizabeth Goodspeed — Designer, art director & writer

- **Live:** https://elizabethgoodspeed.com/
- **Surfaced through:** design/writing community discovery; live site and current role verified directly
- **Why it is cool:** The homepage behaves like an editorial flat plan. A dense project index, newsletter/writing links and an irregular image field let visitors skim by title or visual texture. The work images retain their odd formats—packaging scans, book spreads, artifacts—rather than being normalized into identical cards. Her role sentence appears after the visual evidence, and “Recent work” / “Let’s chat” provide a clean way out. The site feels archival and contemporary at once because the content, not chrome, supplies the color and eccentricity.
- **Lands with:** **HR and aesthetic-minded founders first** (taste is undeniable), **product leads second** (strong curation and information design, less explicit product impact).
- **Reference value for Garvit:** A reminder that a PM’s reading, writing, teardown or artifact collection could become visual evidence of taste—without pretending those things are product case studies.

## D. Studio OS / Personal Universe

These treat the site itself as a system. They are more expressive, so the useful lesson is selective: borrow the organizing metaphor, not all the motion or density.

### 9. Jin Su Park — Multidisciplinary product designer & creative developer

- **Live:** https://jinsupark.com/
- **Surfaced via:** [Dark.design](https://www.dark.design/website/jin-su-park)
- **Why it is cool:** “Oasis One” turns a personal portfolio into a small studio operating system. A live status header shows cities/time, a commit-style line (“last commit 8 hours ago”) makes the site feel maintained, and filters split the archive into Product, AI, Brand, Graphic, Web and Creative. Sixty-plus projects are numbered with terse action titles—“Reimagining Devin AI,” “Building a Personal Memory Bank”—while the hero reduces the philosophy to “Make great things, feel amazing.” The contrast between precise database structure and emotive copy is the distinctive move.
- **Lands with:** **Product/design leads first** (systems thinking and range), **startup founders second** (AI/current-tech fluency), **HR third** (the archive can be cognitively heavy).
- **Reference value for Garvit:** Useful for a PM “decision archive” or filterable body of work. Do not copy the volume; three strong filters and six items would communicate more cleanly.

### 10. Carson Fidje — Graphic designer

- **Live:** https://carsonfidje.com/
- **Surfaced via:** [Behance](https://www.behance.net/gallery/244043479/Personal-Website) and [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/graphic_design/comments/1mp4pzo)
- **Why it is cool:** Carson explicitly designed the portfolio as a personal brand system and organized it by experience rather than category. Motion, pacing and hierarchy are meant to feel like walking through a studio, and the site extends into music/inspiration writing rather than stopping at work thumbnails. Reddit commenters specifically remembered the music and inspiration section and described him as someone they would enjoy working with—a useful proof that the personal layer lands.
- **Lands with:** **HR/creative recruiters first** (memorable and culturally fluent), **creative leads second**, **startup founders/product leads third** (the introductory gate was criticized for not saying quickly enough what he does).
- **Reference value for Garvit:** Mine the music/inspiration-as-authored-editorial idea and the studio pacing. Do **not** copy the ambiguous intro gate or hover treatment that hides the work; those were the main founder/reviewer objections.

## Cross-reference judgment

Across Reddit feedback, the consistent line is useful for the next round: personality helps only after role, scope and evidence are obvious. Reviewers praised memorable quirks, but repeatedly rejected portfolios where animation hid the work, case studies became long books, or the hero failed the ten-second “why hire this person?” test.

The strongest combined reference stack for Garvit is therefore:

- **Boris Verks** for outcomes and PM-shaped proof.
- **Paco Lui** for one optional, genuinely delightful interaction.
- **Molly Mielke McCarthy** for concise founder-grade biography.
- **Alejandro Verdeja** for serious-work / human-side-project balance.
- **Justin Farrugia** for utility microinteractions and startup immediacy.
- A light dose of **Elizabeth Goodspeed** for editorial curation and cultural taste.

This is a scouting recommendation only. It intentionally stops before layout, palette, type or interaction design decisions.
